Which Way Did It Move?
Diagnosing and Overcoming Directional Motion Blindness in Video-LLMs

The problem

One clip, two questions

A yellow ball moves to the right. We show the clip to a Video-LLM and ask about its color, then about its direction.

QWhat color is it?

Video-LLM
Yellow correct

QWhich way is it moving?

Video-LLM
Leftward ground truth: rightward

The model names the color and gets the direction wrong. We call this directional motion blindness.

Benchmark

Even frontier models are near chance

With four possible directions, guessing scores 25%. Every model recognizes the color, and none of them reliably tells the direction.

Diagnosis

They see it. They just don't say it.

Is the direction lost somewhere inside the model? We follow the signal down the pipeline and test every stage with a linear probe.

Just fine-tune it?

Works on simple scenes. Fades on real ones.

Instruction tuning on synthetic direction questions closes the gap where it was trained. As the scene gets visually richer, the direction signal weakens and accuracy falls.

simplevisually complex

Direction accuracy of LLaVA-Video-7B after instruction tuning on MoDirect-Inst.

The model learned to answer. So why does the answer fade on harder scenes?

Why does it fade?

Shared orientation, not enough magnitude

At the readout we extract a direction concept vector for each domain: the internal signal that says which way the object moved. Then we compare the four domains.

Step 1

Instruction tuning aligns the orientation

We tune on Primitive-on-Syn only. Afterwards the concept vectors of all four domains point the same way, with late-layer cosine similarity above 0.9. Without tuning, cross-domain similarity is much lower.

Step 2

Same orientation, weaker magnitude

Along that shared orientation, the vector gets shorter as the scene gets visually richer. Accuracy falls with it.

Step 3

Restore the magnitude, and the binding gap narrows

We stretch each vector back to the Primitive-on-Syn magnitude. Orientation is untouched and nothing is retrained, yet the model binds the signal to the right answer far more often.

Vector directions and lengths are illustrative. Accuracies are from the paper's magnitude intervention on the instruction-tuned LLaVA-Video-7B.

The failure is a magnitude deficit, not missing geometry. We need a stronger direction signal, before it enters the LLM.

Method

DeltaDirect: teach the projector to carry motion

To strengthen the signal at its source, we subtract the projector features of two adjacent frames. A lightweight motion head has to predict the 2-D motion vector from that difference, so the features themselves must carry direction.

Result

Training-only. Zero cost at inference.

The model runs exactly as before, with the same inputs and the same tokens. Direction accuracy rises on synthetic clips, and on real-world videos it never trained on.
